Governance · The founding decision

We ran it on ourselves first.

On 29 August 2026, in one working day, Applied Identities became its own first governance client. We compiled a conduct kit (a hash-pinned mandate rulebook for what our agent fleet may decide) through Mandate Studio, our intake compiler. Our founder adopted that kit as the founding entry of our decision ledger, an append-only record of material decisions. He signed the adoption with a key our organization publicly attests he holds. The record was certified, audited, and anchored to a public blockchain the same afternoon. Everything public in that story you can check yourself, including the founding decision itself; the one part you must take from us is labeled as ours below.

The claim

Our founding decision is “signed by an authenticated principal whose key and role are attested by their employer, the accountable legal entity.”

That is the whole claim, and not more. A principal here is the accountable human, never a system. We do not claim the signer's identity was verified by an outside party; that is a later stage of this work, and we say so plainly.

Verify this yourself

Five checks. No account, no permission from us.

1 · Resolve our organization's DID

A cryptographic identifier served from our own domain: /.well-known/did.json

Proves the public key named there belongs to did:web:appliedidentities.com.

2 · Check the domain-linkage credential

/.well-known/did-configuration.json

Proves the DID and the https domain vouch for each other, signed by that same key.

3 · Read the employer binding

A signed public statement of who holds which key, in which role: /.well-known/vi/bindings/daniel-k1/v1.json

Proves the organization attests that Daniel, as founder, holds the daniel-k1 principal key. Validity 2026-08-29 to 2027-08-29.

4 · Confirm the anchor on Base

An hourly fingerprint of the ledger head, written to Base mainnet (chain id 8453). Contract 0xE4a7CfC6C80754c349C1366f6ec1b7E0B5E74516, transaction 0xca2310774d854def670e85aa283dbbfcd2aededaeb0ca691691a47c1856a8cf5, block 50615138, 29 August 2026, 16:07 UTC.

Proves the ledger state containing the founding decision existed by that block, and any later rewrite would break the anchored head.

5 · Open the founding decision itself

/.well-known/vi/records/dec-edb24f30….json

Read the resolution, the options that were on the table, and the principal. Verify the co-sign against the principal key published in the binding from step 3, and the binding's own signature against the DID key from step 1. Proves the content of the founding decision, not just its existence.

Our attestation

The five steps above are checks you run. This part you take from us, and we label it as ours. Our closing audit, verifying signatures against public key material only, reported (condensed from the audit output):

chain
20 record(s) VERIFIED
the founding decision
dec-edb24f30-a262-45ba-882b-44f644f51a01 VERIFIED daniel as founder @ urn:applied-identities:legal-entity:prototype, binding daniel-k1
summary
1 verified · 2 stage-1 (integrity only) · 0 failed

Why the first certified decision sits at record 20: the chain already held 19 records; this is its first certified decision, and the earlier records predate the binding and remain integrity-only by design. And “prototype” in the entity identifier is deliberate: the legal entity is a variable of each engagement's intake, not a constant of the system.

What this proves, and what it does not

The anchor proves “tamper evidence, existence in time, no split view.” It does not prove “that the record was true when written.” We keep that line on every surface this system touches, because a ledger that overstates itself is worse than no ledger.

Beyond the founding record published above, the decision records themselves are not publicly readable. The full-chain audit is ours to run, not yet yours to reproduce, and we say so rather than imply otherwise.
